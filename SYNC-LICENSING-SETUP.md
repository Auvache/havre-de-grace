# Sync Licensing Setup

A working checklist for getting the Havre De Grace catalog ready for sync. Check boxes off as they're done.

_Started October 2, 2026. Revised November 4, 2026: Songtradr closed its artist marketplace on September 30, 2026; there are no separate vocal and instrument tracks; the steps are grouped into fewer, bigger tasks._

## The goal

Own 100% of every master and every song, so each track is **one-stop**: one person can say yes to a license. That's the biggest advantage an independent artist has in sync. Pay the people who helped generously anyway.

## Already in place

- DistroKid distribution
- BMI: Stefan as writer, self-published (no publishing company). BMI pays self-published works' writer and publisher shares to the writer, so nothing is lost. Released songs are registered as works.
- MLC account
- YouTube Content ID is off on every release (no Social Media Pack). Keep it that way: Music Vine and most libraries won't take songs that are registered with Content ID elsewhere.

## The checklist

### 1. Collect the royalties you're already owed

- [ ] **SoundExchange:** register as both featured artist and rights owner, then add the 18 ISRCs below. It's free, and it pays for plays on Pandora, SiriusXM and internet radio, which DistroKid doesn't collect. Unclaimed money is held for about three years.
- [ ] **MLC:** join the **Member Hub** as a self-administered songwriter, as yourself (no company needed). Then register all 18 works: you as writer, 100%, IPI 01363806247, plus each song's ISRC. The MLC user account already exists; the Hub doesn't yet.

### 2. Ownership agreements with Parker, Sam and Josh

Every one of the 18 songs has Parker on it, so **nothing goes to a library until this is done**. That includes the debut.

- [ ] Finalize the three agreements: thank-you amounts, legal names, and the Songtradr mention swapped for "a music library or agent"
- [ ] Talk with all three. Ask Parker for the 24-bit master WAVs of all 18 songs and a copy of the session folders.
- [ ] All three signed. Store the PDFs next to the master WAVs.

### 3. Files

- [ ] Master WAVs for all 18 songs in one sync folder, backed up. The masters aren't on this Mac; only the site's MP3s are.
- [ ] Instrumentals: test AI separation on two or three songs, keep only the ones that sound clean, and mark the rest as "no instrumental" (Claude runs the separation; you listen)
- [ ] For each instrumental kept: add an `(Instrumental)` alternate title to that song's work in BMI

### 4. Metadata sheet (Claude builds this)

- [ ] Build one row per song: title, album, ISRC, BMI work number, writer IPI, splits, length, BPM, key, 3–5 mood words, lyrics, instrumental (y/n) and contributors owed a share
- [ ] Fill in the BMI work numbers from the BMI portal (the writer IPI is already recorded below)

### 5. Get represented

Apply to the curated libraries first. Don't put any song anywhere else until you know whether a library wants it exclusively.

- [ ] **Musicbed:** email the team with *Into the Wild* and the best of the debut. Get the split and exclusivity terms in writing before signing.
- [ ] **Music Vine:** apply. It's strong with wedding and documentary filmmakers, which suits intimate acoustic songs.
- [ ] **Marmoset:** submit during its monthly window
- [ ] Record who took what, on what terms, in the metadata sheet

### 6. Every release from now on

- [ ] Record a guitar-only pass after each keeper take, so every new song has a real instrumental
- [ ] Get the contributor agreement signed **before** the session
- [ ] Keep the master WAV, plus the instrumental, when mixing is finished
- [ ] Send new songs to the libraries that took you, in the week after release
- [ ] Yearly tally: DistroKid per-song earnings plus the year's sync fees, then a statement and payment to each contributor

### What the agreements give everyone

Each contributor gives you full ownership of the recordings and no claim on the songs, and nobody can block a license. In return they get a share of what the **recording** earns: the master side of sync fees, plus DistroKid streaming and download earnings. Songwriting income stays 100% yours.

Example: a $2,000 license for Goodbye, Norma Jeane is about $1,000 master side and $1,000 publishing. Parker gets $200, Sam and Josh $100 each, and you keep $1,600.

They also get credit wherever the song is credited, use of the recordings in their own portfolios, a yearly statement with payment (amounts under $20 carry over), and a one-off thank-you at signing. The drafts are in `~/Downloads`. There's no lawyer review; the agreements are kept short and plain on purpose.

## Later, not now

- **Pitching music supervisors directly** (DISCO playlists, one song per email). Do this once there are instrumentals and a library placement or two to point to.
- **A licensing page or a `licensing@` email alias.** `/contact` already says "booking, press, licensing", which is enough for now.
- **A publishing company:** only if a library, publisher or foreign society asks for one. It doesn't raise BMI or MLC income.
- **DistroKid Splits** for any song that starts earning real money. The agreements already allow it.
- **Business setup:** a separate checking account for music, asking your tax preparer about Schedule C, keeping receipts, and umbrella insurance. Form an LLC only after a sync deal of $5,000 or more, steady music income, or a library or publisher that wants a company. Trademark "Havre De Grace" when the name matters.

_General guidance, not legal advice._

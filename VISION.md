# Vision: The Productive Creative

*A working note from planning conversations, 2026-09-30 and 2026-10-02. To be continued.*

## The idea

Havre De Grace is my outlet, not a plan to get famous. I have no choice but to play the guitar and write songs. Music keeps flowing out of me, and this project is where it goes. That's the hook.

I would love to do this full time eventually, and I'm in no rush. I want to make money, and I think the best way to do that is to make decisions about myself and the fans, not about the money.

Line upon line, step by step. I build it the way I build software: in small increments, shipped often, adding up over years. My credibility comes from being creative and productive. It shows in the body of work, not in hits.

## Why productivity

1. **I'm a software engineer.** I think about efficiency and incremental development all the time, so I apply it to my art.
2. **My constraints require it.** I have a full-time job, a young family, and sometimes only 30 minutes a day. If I want to keep creating, the time has to be used well.

## What I make

- **Records:** about one album a year, plus 1–2 EPs, for as long as the music keeps coming. That's roughly 18–20 finished songs a year.
- **Stories:** part of how I write songs is making up stories as allegories for what I'm feeling, then putting those arcs and characters into the songs. Some of those stories deserve to be told in full. *Scarecrow*, from the first album, is a story I'd love to write as a novella someday.
- **A record of the work:** a 15–20 minute film about recording and producing the third album.

Songs, stories, and the film about making them all come from the same place. That's the productive creative.

## Where it could go

*(Less important than the daily practice. The direction matters more than the destination.)*

- Ten years of steady releases: around ten albums and a stack of EPs, a catalog that keeps compounding.
- A few novellas or short-story collections tied to songs.
- A short documentary for each album, or at least the ones with a story worth telling.
- An audience that grows slowly because there's always something new and the work is honest.

### Models

People who made a large body of work alongside a day job or tight constraints:

- **Anthony Trollope** wrote for three hours every morning, 250 words per quarter hour, before going to his job at the Post Office. He published 47 novels.
- **Robert Pollard** (Guided by Voices) taught fourth grade while writing and recording a huge catalog lo-fi at home.
- **Charles Ives** ran an insurance agency and composed on nights and weekends.
- **Ryan Holiday** shows how to build something you own, add to it daily, and bet on work that lasts (*Perennial Seller*).

## The daily practice

*(Draft. This is the main thing to get right.)*

Treat it like a development workflow: a small backlog, a strict limit on work in progress, and something moving forward every day.

**Every day: one hour for the project**
1. **Play first, 15 minutes minimum.** This is the anchor, and it doesn't have to produce anything. On a short day, playing is the whole session, and that counts.
2. **Then move one thing forward** in that day's lane. Only one item is active per lane:
   - *Music:* the song currently being written, tracked, or mixed.
   - *Prose:* the current story, starting with *Scarecrow*.
3. **Log one line.** Date and what moved, like a commit message. The log keeps me honest, and it becomes the narrative spine of the album film.

**Every week: seven hours, split by lane**
- **Music: 4 sessions.** Writing, tracking, or mixing, depending on the phase of the year. Camera rolling at every tracking session: one session produces the album, footage for the film, and Shorts.
- **Prose: 2 sessions.** About 45 minutes of writing after playing, roughly 1,000 words a week. During the April–May release crunch, drop to 1 session.
- **Promotion and review: 1 session.** Queue Shorts from the week's footage, check the release schedule, and pick next week's tasks.

**Every year: a release on June 6, my birthday**
Set the release date and work backward from it. EPs come from the overflow: songs that don't fit the album, alternate takes, covers, maybe Swedish-language songs.

### Album 3 schedule (release June 6, 2027)

| When | Music lane | Done when |

Release on June 6 itself, whatever day of the week it falls on. "A new record every year on my birthday" is a ritual fans can count on, and it fits a project that's an outlet rather than a campaign. Let the date tell the story; it doesn't need to be framed as a stand against Friday releases.

### *Scarecrow* (prose lane)

- **Target length:** about 25,000 words, a range of 20,000–30,000. That's novella length (*The Metamorphosis* is ~22k words, *The Old Man and the Sea* ~27k, *Of Mice and Men* ~30k). It's short enough to finish and long enough for fans to get lost in.
- **Pace:** 1,000 words a week means a first draft in about 25 weeks, around April 2027. Then a slower revision over the summer.
- **Release it as a full book, not a serial.** The song is already its teaser. Aim for the half of the year opposite the album (around December 2027), so the book and the album each get their own moment.

## Income

**The filter:** an income stream fits if it comes from work I'm already making, gives fans something they'd want anyway, and doesn't add a weekly obligation.

- **Sync licensing (Songtradr, Musicbed).** Every song released is another chance at a placement, at almost no extra cost. Record one guitar-only pass after each keeper take, because live takes have vocal bleed and music supervisors want instrumentals. Submit each album's songs and instrumentals in the week after release.
- **Performances.** The lazy man's tour each summer after the album comes out: venue shows and opening slots, with the venue providing sound.
- **Direct sales.** Bandcamp for downloads and, eventually, physical copies.
- **Merch (Printful/Printify on the site).** Print-on-demand, so no inventory. Make it from the work itself: album art and stills from the SVG films, lyric sheets, and later the *Scarecrow* book.
- **Books.** *Scarecrow* and any later stories.
- **What doesn't fit:** anything that runs on a content schedule (subscription tiers that require regular posts), or anything that changes the music to suit a market.

Track the income once a year, at the June 6 release. Going full time is a milestone to reach someday, not a deadline.

## What to protect

It's an outlet first. If the schedule, metrics, or comments start turning the anchor into a job, the practice gives way, not the other way around. Keep some songwriting that's never meant for release. A missed day is fine. The log isn't a streak to protect, just a record.

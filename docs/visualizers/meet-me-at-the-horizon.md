# Meet Me at the Horizon — visualizer

Track 7. **No film yet**: [/visualizers/meet-me-at-the-horizon](/visualizers/meet-me-at-the-horizon) shows the proposed stills, and this file holds the words for them. The song's core idea, from the brief: *Sleepless nights*.

The stills are drawn by `tools/video-styles/visualizers/meet-me-at-the-horizon.mjs` (and, for Lying Awake, `horizon.mjs` over `shared/video/films/horizon.mjs`) and published with `SITE=1 node tools/video-styles/visualizers/build.mjs`. Every still is a real moment of the record (song seconds below). A chosen idea becomes a film in `shared/video/films/` in the Into the Wild maze format: see [README.md](README.md).

## Lying Awake: three directions (the newer round)

One night, lying awake, from the first line to the dawn the song keeps reaching for. Three ways to draw it, as stills to choose from before anything moves: a star atlas where every line he sings joins stars into a figure, the same atlas with nightmares in the silences, and a sleep lab's EEG chart. The gaps the nightmares live in are the song's own, found by `horizonGaps()` in the measured score.

**Recommended: Nightmares.** It is Constellations with a third act: the verses race (the red sparks), every silence turns the sky on him, the next word jolts him awake, and only after the last "meet me at the horizon" does the dawn win. Constellations is the gentler version of the same piece. Monkey Brain is the clearest picture of not being able to switch off, but it is a sleep chart, not the sky and the horizon the song is named for.

### Nightmares

The star atlas, with a third act. The verses keep him awake: every line that names a thing joins stars into it, and red sparks jump between stars on the sung words. But in every real silence in the singing (the intro, and six gaps of two to three seconds, measured from the record) the sky reddens and every figure he has drawn turns into its bad version, relabelled in red: the sheep a wolf, the moon a blood moon, the bed a coffin, the eye many eyes, someone standing in the door of the house, a hand through the window, a black sun, a dead tree, the clock spinning. The longest gap comes straight after the first "meet me at the horizon": the lover turns away and the ground cracks. The next sung word jolts him awake, a white flash and a shake, and everything snaps back. After the last "meet me at the horizon" no nightmare comes: the sun comes up behind the two of them and he sleeps.

### Constellations

A star-atlas plate of the night sky over a black ridge. Every line that names a thing joins stars that were already there into it, drawn by a red pen on the line's first word and labelled like an atlas, in Latin: the sheep he isn't counting, the moon, the bed, an hourglass, the clock, the tree, the dawn, the eye, the spine, the house, the window, him alone. Each holds and fades to a ghost, so the sky fills with everything he has thought about tonight. Red sparks jump between stars on the sung words, the racing mind, thick in the verses. Gold comes up behind the ridge in each chorus, higher each time. On the first "meet me at the horizon" two figures stand on the ridge, not yet touching; on the last, his red and her gold, their hands meet, and the stars go out into the morning.

### Monkey Brain

A sleep lab's EEG chart: the head from above, nineteen electrodes on the 10–20 layout, the technician's notes beside it. His mind is a red point swinging from electrode to electrode on every sung word, a big swing on every line, and every swing leaves a pencil arc, so by the end the head is covered in the night's scribble. The technician notes what each line was about. The hypnogram under it is stuck at WAKE: it slips towards sleep only in the gaps and is pulled straight back up by the next thought. In the outro the swings stop, the window turns gold and the trace drops to N3.

## The first five ideas

### A. The Sleep Study

> When the absence of light doesn't mean that I sleep

One night on a polysomnogram: the stages of sleep as rows, the hours of the night across the sheet. The red pen is scored awake on every sung line and only starts to go under in the gaps between them.

**Moves.** The trace writes left to right across the whole song, stepping on the measured words. The light channel above it turns gold in each chorus, then back to black. In the outro, the first long gap of the night, the trace finally drops to deep sleep as the morning comes.

**Red.** The pen and its trace, the only thing moving across the chart.

### B. Star Trails

> I lie in my bed and I think of the sky

A long exposure of the night sky over a black hill. The stars turn round the pole and draw their arcs as the song goes, so the length of every trail is how long he has been lying awake.

**Moves.** All the arcs lengthen together, smoothly, from the first line to the last. Gold dawn lines rise behind the ridge in each chorus and sink back. In the outro they climb and the stars fade into them.

**Red.** One star whose trail falls through the night and touches the horizon on the last "meet me at the horizon".

### C. No Counting Sheep

> Just me and my thoughts, no counting sheep

The night counted the way days are scratched on a cell wall: one stroke for every sung word, in fives, filling the plate row by row. It is the most literal picture of a night that will not end.

**Moves.** Each stroke is drawn on its word’s measured onset in about 90 ms, so the wall fills in time with the singing and stops in the gaps. A window of gold light crosses the wall in each chorus and settles on it in the outro.

**Red.** The newest stroke, always the one just sung.

### D. The Ceiling

> And the room breaks the silence when the house starts to creak

The view from the pillow: the ceiling, its rose, the walls falling away at the edges. Moonlight through the window sits on the ceiling as a pale shape, and a hairline crack spreads out from the cornice.

**Moves.** The window’s light slides slowly across the ceiling for the whole night and turns gold and wide in the dawns. The crack grows a little on every sung word, so it spreads with the worry and pauses when he does.

**Red.** The crack, worrying its way across the ceiling.

### E. Two Lights

> Meet me at the horizon

One horizon line over a dark engraved sea, with two lights on it: his in red from the left and yours in gold from the right. The whole video is whether they meet.

**Moves.** The lights close through the verses and nearly touch on the first "meet me at the horizon". They drift apart again while he misses the way things used to be, then meet on the last one. The water trembles on every word and the night lines thin as the dawn comes.

**Red.** His light, travelling the horizon towards hers.

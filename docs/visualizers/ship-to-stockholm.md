# Ship to Stockholm — visualizer

Track 9. **No film yet**: [/visualizers/ship-to-stockholm](/visualizers/ship-to-stockholm) shows the proposed stills, and this file holds the words for them. The song's core idea, from the brief: *Moving on*.

The stills are drawn by `tools/video-styles/visualizers/ship-to-stockholm.mjs` and published with `SITE=1 node tools/video-styles/visualizers/build.mjs`. Every still is a real moment of the record (song seconds below). A chosen idea becomes a film in `shared/video/films/` in the Into the Wild maze format: see [README.md](README.md).

## Five ideas

### A. Looking Astern

> Oh, away with the land and away with the light

The view off the back of the ship for the whole song: the coast and its lighthouse getting smaller on the horizon, the wake running back to it. Moving on, seen from the only place you can watch it happen.

**Moves.** The water streams away to the horizon at a steady speed and swells on the words. The land shrinks all song, the lighthouse beam weakens, and in the outro the coast slips under the horizon and is gone.

**Red.** The wake’s centre line: the way already come, a red thread from the stern back to the land.

### B. The Departure Board

> The ship back to Stockholm, it waits on no man

A split-flap board at the quay. Every ship on it leaves on time, verse by verse, and the board rolls up. Stockholm is the last row.

**Moves.** The clock keeps running. At each section’s end a row flips to DEPARTED and the board glides up a line. On the last line Stockholm flips to FINAL CALL, then DEPARTED on the word “Stockholm”, and the board is left empty.

**Red.** Stockholm’s row, climbing the board as the ships ahead of it go.

### C. The Lead Through the Ice

> It’s deep water rescue on razor thin ice

Pack ice from directly above. A lead of open water cracks through it, corner to corner, and the floes shoulder apart to let the ship by.

**Moves.** The crack advances with the words sung and keeps creeping through the instrumentals. Floes near the head jolt on each word and drift wider the longer the lead has been open. By the last line the lead has crossed the whole plate.

**Red.** The ship’s track up the lead, with the bow as a dot at its head.

### D. Telegraph Tape

> I can’t seem to reach you, not by letter or phone

A Morse register printing the lyric as it is sung, word by word, onto paper tape, with the operator’s pencilled letters underneath. Message after message goes out and none of it is answered.

**Moves.** The tape advances only when a word is sung, so the spacing on it is the rhythm of the voice, and the instrumentals leave long blank runs. The spool of blank tape thins over the song. It ends on S-T-O-C-K-H-O-L-M.

**Red.** The word being sent now. Once it has been sent it turns to ink.

### E. A Tightrope to Eden

> We’re walking a tightrope to Eden

One rope from a mast to a rock with a single tree on it, strung above deep water. A red bead makes the crossing over the whole song.

**Moves.** The bead moves with the words and the clock and never stops. Its weight bends the rope, and every sung syllable sends a ripple down the line. It reaches the tree on the last line.

**Red.** The bead: the one crossing, left to right.

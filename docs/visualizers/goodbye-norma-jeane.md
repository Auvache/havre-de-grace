## What it does

The mark drawn on, the title drawn on, then the black lifts off a dark
picture-house letter board. It turns over into NORMA JEANE as the voice comes
in, then flips through all twenty-nine of her films in release order, and spins
down to nothing after Something's Got to Give. On the last hit, the mark. Her
name is never spelled, but three times it shows through the scramble in red.

The board follows the song. In the verses a title lands on every phrase, every
four bars; in the pre-choruses and breaks, every two. In the bar where the
record drops out before a chorus, the board flips blank and the bulbs go out.
Then the chorus answers itself: GOODBYE on "Goodbye", NORMA on "Norma", JEANE on
"Jean", and on "more, more, more" the board turns to MORE a row at a time.

## Decided by the user (2026-11-02)

- **Split flaps** beat two alternatives tried the same day, slot-machine reels
  and letters hung by hand: "A is definitely best, especially when well timed
  with the music." Timing to the record is what they value most.
- **The board does more by section, and the frame stays fixed** (chosen over
  camera moves or a picture-house drawing): verses every 4 bars, pre-choruses
  and breaks every 2, a blank-and-dark drop bar before each chorus, and the
  chorus answering its own words.
- **Her name is never spelled in full.** Keep the three partial red clues (3, 6
  and 9 letters); confirmed over landing MARILYN MONROE once or on every chorus.
- Any new board event must land on a measured word or downbeat.

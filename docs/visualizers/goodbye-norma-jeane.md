# Goodbye, Norma Jeane — the marquee

Track 3. [/visualizers/goodbye-norma-jeane](/visualizers/goodbye-norma-jeane).
Film: `shared/video/films/marquee.mjs` (`marqueeFrame`, `marqueeMoments`).
Score: `app/config/goodbyeNormaJeaneScore.ts`. The whole film from 2026-11-02.

## What it does

The mark drawn on, the title drawn on, then the black lifts off a
picture-house letter board already twinkling. It turns over into NORMA JEANE
as the voice comes in, then surfaces all twenty-nine of her films in release
order, one at a time, each assembling out of spinning letters and coming
apart again, and spins down to nothing after Something's Got to Give. On the last hit, the mark. Her
name is never spelled, but three times it shows through the scramble in red.

The board follows the song. In the verses a title lands on every phrase, every
four bars; in the pre-choruses and breaks, every two. It is whole only for a
beat or two on that downbeat: the rest of its slot it is surfacing or coming
apart. Nothing on the sign is ever still: held letters turn over onto
themselves and letters blink in the empty tiles. In the bar where the
record drops out before a chorus, the board flips blank and the bulbs go out.
Then the chorus answers itself: GOODBYE on "Goodbye", NORMA on "Norma", JEANE on
"Jean", and on "more, more, more" the board turns to MORE a row at a time.

## Decided by the user (2026-11-02)

- **Split flaps** beat two alternatives tried the same day, slot-machine reels
  and letters hung by hand: "A is definitely best, especially when well timed
  with the music." Timing to the record is what they value most.
- **The board does more by section, and the frame stays fixed** (chosen over
  camera moves or a picture-house drawing): verses every 4 bars, pre-choruses
  and breaks every 2, a blank-and-dark drop bar before each chorus, and the
  chorus answering its own words.
- **Her name is never spelled in full.** Keep the three partial red clues (3, 6
  and 9 letters); confirmed over landing MARILYN MONROE once or on every chorus.
- Any new board event must land on a measured word or downbeat.

## The twinkle (decided 2026-11-05)

Asked for "more letters and movement" while a title is up. Of five motion
tests (twinkle, sung words in the gaps, five rows with key words, a chase
wave, a readerboard strip) the user chose the twinkle: "A is the best". While
a board is held, letters blink on and off in the tiles it leaves empty, each
flip on an eighth, busier in pre-choruses and choruses. The others were
deleted. What held, and still holds:

- Extra motion only while a board is held, never in a turn or a drop bar, so
  the scrambles and the dark bar still read as events.
- Twinkle letters are never red: red still only ever means her.
- Nothing twinkles beside a lit letter in its row: a stray letter next to a
  title reads as part of it ("AS YOU PUT").

## The dance (decided 2026-11-05)

Asked: "letters can be constantly flipping, even when a title is present…
dancing letters from the outset, like it started before the fade." A held
board's own letters turn over onto themselves on the eighths, a few at a time
(they keep their red), and the empty board twinkles from 0:00 under the black,
so it is already going when the film fades up.

## The surfacing (decided 2026-11-05)

Asked: "a more gradual build up/break down of each of the titles in order to
fill the space… the title can slowly reveal itself by letters slowly falling
into place. The actual title doesn't need to last as a whole that long. It
will more so bubble up to the surface, than disappear as the letters flip
away." The user chose, from three options each: letters **lock out of the
spin**, a **slow build and quick break**, and **one title at a time**.

- A title's tiles start spinning through random letters in the first third of
  its slot, then lock onto their letters a few at a time, scattered, on the
  eighths; the last locks on the downbeat the title used to land on (about
  two-thirds of the slot).
- It is whole for a beat (two in the verses), then its letters flip away,
  scattered, on the sixteenths, over as long again; the board twinkles empty
  for an eighth or so before the next one starts. Titles never overlap.
- The first two clues happen inside a build: her place spins with the title's
  tiles and her letters show through in red for a beat and a half before any
  title letter locks. The third is still the scramble into the last chorus.
- In a chorus the hook holds two bars and the title surfaces over the next
  two, whole on the bar before "more, more, more" (it was a two-beat turn on
  "I've been in love", then two bars of an empty board).
- The title after each drop bar still slams down on the downbeat, as the
  record comes back; it comes apart like the rest.
- The only words on the board: the titles, NORMA JEANE at the start, and the
  chorus's own (GOODBYE / NORMA JEANE on the words, MORE).

Tried and dropped the same day: key words of the lyric slammed up on their
syllables, half-length titles, and ten headlines from her life in date order
between the films ("the historical events don't fit the theme").

## Still, ringed, after the fade (decided 2026-11-05)

- **Whole and still for a second.** A title holds completely still for three
  eighths (about a second) before it comes apart; its letters no longer turn
  over while whole.
- **The twinkle starts after the fade**, on the downbeat at bar 5 (13.3 s),
  about a second after the black lifts. NORMA JEANE now surfaces out of
  spinning letters like a title, from that same downbeat.
- **A clear ring.** Once a title is half locked, the tiles beside it and
  directly above and below its letters stay clear, so it reads however busy
  the board is; once it has gone, its tiles are free. A ring that included
  the corners left almost nothing free on a three-row board (NORMA JEANE alone
  blocked every tile).

## Intensity tests (2026-11-05, pending a pick)

Asked: "for more intense parts of the music (e.g. the instrumental trumpet
section or choruses 'you wanted more'), make the flashing more intense… maybe
even filling the whole board. For softer parts (between singing) have fewer
tiles twinkling." Three tests on the page (`?test=measured`, `sections`,
`pulse`; sheets `FILM=marquee-measured` etc.). In all three a title's spinning
tiles follow the same level: soft, they turn over less and often come up
blank; loud, they spin hard. Current is the old twinkle, set by section only.

| | How the level is set | What it looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Follows the record | `score.brightness`, a digit a beat | Tiles light, change letter and go out on the eighths; a few in the verse gaps, most of the free board in the choruses and the trumpet |
| Set by section | By hand from the score: verse gaps 5%, sung verse 25%, pre-chorus 30–45%, breaks 30%, choruses 70%, full from each "you wanted (more)" for two bars and on the trumpet (bars 78–82) | The same motion, in clear steps |
| Flashes on the beat | The brightness | On each beat a share of the free tiles flashes for an eighth, the downbeats most; at the brightest nearly the whole board blinks in time |

Measuring intensity: loudness was no use, since the master spans 5 dB from the
quietest bar to the loudest. Energy above 1.8 kHz spans 10 dB and follows the
voice and the brass. `tools/video-styles/brightness.mjs` prints it a digit a
beat; it lives in the score as `brightness`.
